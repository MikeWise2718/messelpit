# Nix (2003) — engineering geology of the Messel pit: notes for scene modeling

Source: **Nix, Thomas (2003): "Untersuchung der ingenieurgeologischen Verhältnisse der
Grube Messel (Darmstadt) im Hinblick auf die Langzeitstabilität der Grubenböschungen."**
Geologische Abhandlungen Hessen, Band 112, 159 p., 49 figs., 10 plates. Hessisches
Landesamt für Umwelt und Geologie (HLUG), Wiesbaden. ISBN 3-89531-814-0.

The scanned PDF lives at
`dthub/docs/Geol. Abh. Hessen Band 112 Grube Messel Langzeitstabilität Grubenböschungen.pdf`
(66 MB, image scan; text layer extracts cleanly).

It is a doctoral-level engineering-geology monograph. Driving question: are the
former open-pit slopes stable long-term? (Answer: no — every slope fails the DIN 1054
safety factors; mm–cm/year creep continues, episodically reaccelerated by heavy rain,
hence the permanent monitoring program running since 1993.) But roughly half the volume
is reusable groundwork for two planned messelpit modeling efforts:

1. **Per-era pit models** — one model per dated excavation state.
2. **Multi-level "today" model** — the geological layer stack embedded (transparently)
   below the present surface.

## 1. Excavation history — the time slices (Ch. 2.1, 4.1)

### Timeline

| Period | State of the pit |
|---|---|
| 1885–1892 | Industrial mining begins (Gewerkschaft Messel); oil-shale retorting plant built |
| 1889–1924 | Shaft-and-chute mining ("Rolllochbetrieb"); tunnel remnants survive in the pit center at ~90–95 m NN |
| 1913/14 | Electric shovel mining introduced (kept until 1961); mined in ~10 m benches ("Bauscheiben") on numbered levels (Sohlen) |
| 1930/1934 | First documented landslides at the north rim; mining there abandoned |
| **1937** | 4th level reached in the center; W/E/S rims at the 2nd level (mine map exists) |
| **1954** | Official mine survey (Grubenbild, updated to 1971) |
| **1957** | 5th and 6th levels open; W/E/S rim positions already match today's outline (mine map exists) |
| 1961 | Oil extraction ends; mining continues centrally only |
| 1961–1988 | Partial backfilling: SW rim (today's visitor platform) filled to the 3rd level; NW rim (Ytong storage yard) to the 1st level; aerated-concrete rubble heap on the west slope to the 3rd level (~129 m NN, up to 30 m thick) |
| **1971** | Mining ends (Ytong); landfill plans begin |
| 1970s–80s | Landfill preparations: gravel "Schotterfläche" on the 6th level, asphalt "Deponiestraße" on the west rim, re-graded slide masses on the south slope |
| 1987/88 | Landfill permit overturned in court; project abandoned |
| 1992 | Senckenberg becomes trustee operator |
| 1995 | UNESCO World Heritage listing |

### Geometry snapshot sources (what a per-era model would be built from)

- **Mine maps 1937, 1957, 1960** + mine survey 1954 (with additions to 1971), held in
  the archives of the former Bergamt Weilburg / Oberbergamt Wiesbaden
  (file numbers 76 d 543 G, 79 n 0607, 76 d 70-7). Pre-1937 records are lost.
- **Aerial photo series: 1961, 1967, 1971, 1977, 1985, 1986, 1993, 1997**
  (multi-temporal analysis in the study; the 1961 photo is reproduced as Abb. 11,
  Hessisches Landesvermessungsamt series 1058/61, image 114).
- Base map of the study: digitized 1:2000 plan from the 1993/94 pit map (HLUG).

### Key elevations to hang the eras on

- Pit floor today (6th level): **~104 m NN**; rims 160–170 m NN → a 60–70 m deep bowl.
- 3rd level ≈ 129 m NN (top of the rubble heap); shaft-tunnel remnants 90–95 m NN.
- Slope angles: south 30–35°, west 20–30°, east rim a ~10 m near-vertical (to 80°)
  Rotliegend cliff, north 30–50° below a ~100 m wide bench.
- Darmstadt–Dieburg railway runs 30–40 m from the deposit edge at the north rim.

## 2. Stratigraphy — the layer stack for the multi-level model (Ch. 3)

Setting: a **maar lake** formed by a phreatomagmatic eruption ~48 Ma (early Middle
Eocene, Lower Geiseltalium); the lake sediments fill the collapsed diatreme, bounded
by ring faults against the surrounding Paleozoic basement. Bedding is near-horizontal
in the center and steepens to **20–25° dip toward the rims** (everything dips toward
the structural low — this is also the sliding surface of every major landslide).

Top → bottom:

| Unit | Content | Thickness / elevations |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropogenic fills | Spoil, Ytong rubble, gravel drainage layer, slid masses | Rubble heap up to 30 m, to 3rd level ~129 m NN |
| Quaternary | Pleistocene gravel, wind-blown sand | 1–3 m |
| **Upper Messel-Fm** (teoMFo) | Clay/silt/sand, thin brown coal | ~30–35 m, preserved only in three marginal fault troughs ("Mulden"); trough bases at ~140 (S), ~130–135 (SE), ~120–125 (NE) m NN. Almost entirely mined away; small remnants E/SE |
| **Middle Messel-Fm** (teoMFm) | The laminated oil shale (Schwarzpelit) | **~100 m remaining; ~160 m before mining.** Base at ~6 m NN in the structural center (borehole 6/24, FB 2001) |
| (marker bed) | Basin-wide debris-flow sediment separating middle from lower | ~10 m in the center |
| **Lower Messel-Fm** (teoMFu) | Coarse resediments, breccias, turbidites, tuffitic | Up to ~140 m in the center; 40–61 m already at the margins |
| Tuffitic sediments | Transition below the lake fill | to ~240 m depth (FB 2001) |
| Lapilli tuff | Phreatomagmatic eruption products | 240–373 m depth |
| Diatreme breccia | Layered mega-breccia (Rotliegend/granodiorite/amphibolite clasts, 10–25 m beds) | 373–433 m+ (continues ≥50 m below per seismics) |

Base of the Messel-Formation in the structural low: **−129 m NN** (research borehole
FB Messel 2001, 433 m deep, drilled in the pit center — the calibration spine for any
volumetric model).

Surroundings: Rotliegend red sandstones/conglomerates draping the basement
(7–12 m thick S and N, up to ~63 m NE/E, ≥58 m W); below, Paleozoic
granodiorite (N) and diorite (S) with the top 20–25 m weathered to grus; amphibolite
E. Eocene basalt (49 Ma K-Ar) crops out ~500 m NE of the rim.

Material note (why the pit keeps moving): the oil shale is 85–95 % **smectite**
clay plus 20–40 % organic matter (Type-II kerogen, alginite from *Tetraedron minimum*),
strongly swelling/shrinking, water-rich, overconsolidated. Rainfall thresholds that
reactivate the slides: >105 mm/month or >35 mm/72 h.

Useful figures to extract/georeference from the PDF:

- **Abb. 5** — geological map of the pit (1:2000 base).
- **Abb. 6** — NW–SE geological cross-section through the whole deposit, **with the

  the layer model. Section endpoints (GK): R 3482350 / H 5531767 (NW) to

## 3. Borehole data (Ch. 2.2)

- **982 known boreholes** drilled ~1920–2001 in and around Messel.
  - Mining era (~1920–1961): 10 m up to 175.7 m (Mutungsbohrung 1/27); logs by drill
    foremen ("Kohle" = oil shale, "Grus/Granitzersatz" = lower fm or weathered basement).
  - 1961–2001: geotechnical/hydrogeological and research holes, 3 m to 433 m (FB 2001).
- Nix re-interpreted all logs stratigraphically, cross-checked against reflection
  seismics (Buness & Harms 2000, Buness et al. 2003).
- **Caveat:** the actual borehole database (SEP files, layer registers, coordinates for
  all 982 holes) is in the *unpublished* companion report **Nix (2001), "Räumliche
  Modellierung der geologischen und tektonischen Verhältnisse der Grube Messel"**
  (DFG/Senckenberg project) — this volume only summarizes it. For bulk borehole data,
  that report / the HLUG borehole archive / Senckenberg (FIS) are the sources to chase.

## 4. Monitoring network — georeferenced points (Appendix 12.1)

The appendix tabulates every measuring station with **Gauß-Krüger coordinates
(zone 3), ground elevation (m NN), and depth**: 5 deep wells (GW 1–5), ~100 shallow
piezometers (KP 101–525), 28 inclinometers (IN 1–28, 19.5–65 m deep — many sheared
off by the slides, which itself maps the slip surfaces at 7–49 m depth), 5 fixed
geodetic points (F1–F5) and ~35 temporary GPS points.

Parsed into **`docs/messel-nix-2003-stations.csv`** (this repo). Notes:

- CRS is **DHDN / Gauß-Krüger zone 3 (EPSG:31467)** — transform to the messelpit
  frame ETRS89 / UTM 32N (EPSG:25832) before use (well-known datum shift, ~1 m class
  accuracy without a grid file; fine for these purposes).
- Transcribed from OCR of a scanned table; digits were cleaned by hand
  (e.g. "ISO"→150, split digit groups rejoined). Spot-check against the PDF
  (pp. 123–127) before any precision use. Elevations double as ~170 spot heights on
  the 2001 pit topography — a useful sanity check against the DGM1.

## 5. Landslides (Ch. 4–7) — context for the "today" model

All slopes carry documented slide bodies (mapped in Abb. 12): large deep-seated
slides in the Tertiary sediments (N, W, SW, E — active since the 1930s, slip surfaces
preferentially at the lower/middle formation transition, riding the bedding toward the
pit center), rock slides in the Rotliegend/magmatites at the E/SE rims, moving
anthropogenic fills, and creeping historical slide masses. Scarps sit at ~160–170 m NN
at the rims; accumulation zones with pressure ridges at the pit floor. If the "today"
model is ever meant to be morphologically faithful, the hummocky slide topography is a
real feature of the pit, not noise.
